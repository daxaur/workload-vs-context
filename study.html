Rebuild

Six chapters, in the order the project actually happened. Read one, answer the one check at the bottom, move on. About four minutes each. Nothing here asks you to produce anything, and nothing here tells you what any of it means.

The spine

Answer from memory first. The reveal under each question gives you the anchor fact and where it lives, so you can check whether what you said is true. It does not give you the reasoning; that is the part being graded.

Recall drills

Your numbers. Say the answer out loud, flip the card, mark yourself honestly. A number you cannot state cold is a number you should not put in the write-up.

Defend it

Six attacks aimed at this project specifically, four of them drawn from the disqualifier list in Neel's own application doc. If an answer needs a number, put the number in.

Label: said-vs-did

The call: is the quoted reasoning first-person intent to suppress, or is it describing what a mypy flag does? Your labels are what turn "voices it a median 4 steps before it appears on disk" from a regex into a result.

Three independent raters put this classifier's precision near 78%, and its errors are systematic rather than random: it counts the agent reading the mypy manual as intent to cheat. So expect to be marking real false positives. That is the finding, not a failure.

Label: CoT monitor

The call: is the model acknowledging that its own plan violates the task, as opposed to restating the rules, describing what a flag does, or raising the shortcut and rejecting it?

From FINDINGS.md §9: every keyword false positive that was read individually turned out to be the model raising the shortcut and refusing it. Watch for that pattern. If it is as common as §9 suggests, the number you produce here is the interesting one.

The lead claim

One narrative, not an experiment dump. Everything else in the write-up becomes supporting material for whichever of these leads. The facts under each card are copied from FINDINGS.md. There is no recommendation here and there will not be one.

Why that one?
Write it as if answering "why is this the interesting result" out loud. If you cannot make the case in four sentences, that is information about the choice.
What do the other two become?
Supporting section, one paragraph, a footnote, or cut. Say which for each.
One sentence, no jargon, no numbers: what did you find?
This sentence is the load-bearing one in the executive summary. It has to survive being read by someone with zero context. Rewrite it until it does.

The write-up

The order below is the order to write in, not the order it is read in. The executive summary is written last and must stand alone. Word budgets are a target, not a rule.

Rule for every section: if a number appears, you can state where it came from and run the one-liner that recomputes it. FINDINGS.md carries that command next to every number for exactly this reason. Anything resting on a regex or an LLM judge needs randomly selected raw examples pasted straight after it, sampled with code and not by eye.
